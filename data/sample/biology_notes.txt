Biology Sample Notes — Photosynthesis and Cell Biology

PHOTOSYNTHESIS
Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae and some bacteria convert light
energy (usually from the sun) into chemical energy stored as glucose. The overall equation is:
  6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

The process occurs in the chloroplast and has two main stages:
1. Light-dependent reactions (in the thylakoid membrane)
   - Chlorophyll absorbs sunlight.
   - Water molecules are split (photolysis), releasing oxygen as a by-product.
   - ATP and NADPH are produced.
2. Light-independent reactions / Calvin Cycle (in the stroma)
   - CO₂ is fixed using ATP and NADPH.
   - Glucose is synthesised.

Factors that affect the rate of photosynthesis:
  - Light intensity
  - CO₂ concentration
  - Temperature
  - Water availability

CELL BIOLOGY
The cell is the basic unit of life. There are two main cell types:
  - Prokaryotic cells: no membrane-bound nucleus (e.g., bacteria).
  - Eukaryotic cells: have a membrane-bound nucleus (e.g., plants, animals, fungi).

Key organelles in a eukaryotic cell:
  - Nucleus: contains DNA and controls cell activity.
  - Mitochondria: site of aerobic respiration; produces ATP ("powerhouse of the cell").
  - Ribosome: site of protein synthesis.
  - Endoplasmic reticulum (ER): rough ER has ribosomes and is involved in protein processing;
    smooth ER is involved in lipid synthesis.
  - Golgi apparatus: modifies, packages and ships proteins and lipids.
  - Chloroplast (plants only): site of photosynthesis.
  - Vacuole: storage organelle; large central vacuole in plant cells.
  - Cell membrane: phospholipid bilayer controlling what enters and exits the cell.
  - Cell wall (plants, fungi): provides structural support; made of cellulose in plants.

CELL DIVISION
Mitosis produces two genetically identical daughter cells and is used for growth and repair.
Meiosis produces four genetically unique haploid cells and is used for sexual reproduction.

DNA BASICS
DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is the hereditary material in all living organisms.
It is a double helix made of nucleotides. Each nucleotide contains:
  - A phosphate group
  - A deoxyribose sugar
  - A nitrogenous base (A, T, C or G)
Base pairing rules: A pairs with T; C pairs with G.
